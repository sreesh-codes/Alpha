Book

Signed quantities — negative is short. Prices default to the board snapshot; overwrite as they move.
Adding a position books it at the current price — cash moves the other way, so equity does not jump. Marking prices in the Spillover tab is what moves equity. Overwrite cash to match your terminal whenever it drifts. Peak and max drawdown ratchet automatically as net equity moves; if your desk shows a different drawdown, type the desk's number in — the engine is the source of truth, not this page.

Score mechanics

R = N/1,000,000 − 1  ·  S = R / max(D, 5%)  ·  S_final = S − 0.3(n+p)|S|

Margin

E = cash + Σ q·P  ·  G = Σ|q·P|  ·  m = E/G  ·  G ≤ 2E

Stress test

An adverse move of x% on gross notional costs x%·G of equity. Drawdown is measured against your running peak, so gains tighten the percentage you can give back.

Hard stops — agree these before the bell

The first 5% of drawdown is free. Below the floor, S = 20R regardless of how you got there, so the only mistake is leaving risk budget unused. Above it, each extra point of drawdown needs ΔR/R ≥ ΔD/D — the first point past the floor is the most expensive one you will ever buy.
  • DD 4.0% — halve gross exposure. No discussion.
  • DD 5.0% — flat into bonds (0.05% spread, 60M depth) for one full minute.
  • m < 0.45 — reduce. The 0.35 banner is already too late to be comfortable.
  • Never above 1.4× gross. At 2.0× a ~12.5% adverse move on gross force-liquidates you at impact prices.
  • Zero bailouts. −30% of |S| each, and a forced liquidation prints the drawdown that caused it.
  • Risk officer holds an absolute veto. The trader does not argue with it mid-session.

Spillover propagation

Published coefficients: same nation 25% · same sector, other nations 15% · nation in tension 20% inverse · no asset ripples more than 3% from one event. These are deterministic — the edge is reacting to the neighbours before the room does.

Universe

Depth is derived: one order may be at most 20% of depth, so depth = 5 × max order. Volatility class is confirmed for ZAP only — check the board and correct the rest, it changes both spread and depth.

Mean-reversion fade thresholds

Trading pressure decays toward underlying value; news moves value permanently. So the whole discriminator is was there a headline at this timestamp — and headlines are on the board, not your laptop. A deviation is only worth fading once it clears the round trip.
d* = round-trip cost ÷ (1 − 2^(−t/τ))   τ = reversion half-life, t = intended hold

Observed fills

Order splitting

With α < 1 impact is concave, so slicing cuts average slippage. Reality sits between the two bounds below: slices push the price for the ones behind them. The binding limits are 3 orders/sec and the bot watch — slice by hand, at a human rhythm.
One order may be at most 20% of depth. Anything larger is rejected outright, so a big position must be sliced regardless of cost.

Posterior from odds + envelopes

Published odds are your prior. An envelope is noisy evidence — a single one barely moves you, but two that agree move you a lot. That asymmetry is the whole reason to buy on the Back Channel.
P(T=A | E=A) ∝ θ·p_A  ·  P(E=A | T=B) = (1−θ)/(k−1)
Keep a tally all day: envelopes that named the revealed outcome ÷ envelopes seen. θ = 0.5 means an envelope is nearly worthless; θ = 0.8 means one envelope is a real signal. Guessing θ high is the expensive error.

Position sizing against the draw

Enter the move you expect in one asset under each outcome. The tradeable edge is not your expected move — it is the move the room has not priced, so it is measured against the prior, not against zero.
edge = Σ (posterior_i − prior_i) · m_i  ·  max size = free drawdown budget ÷ (|worst move| × price)

Reroll vote

30 seconds, needs Yes from more than half of the houses still in. Silence counts as No. A tie counts as No. The first vote that passes burns the reroll for the entire game.
Expected P&L under a reroll is your current position in the sizing asset above, marked across its outcome moves and weighted by the published prior — a fresh roll is drawn from the same odds, and envelopes are not redone.

Bid ladder

shaded value = V − λ·σ_V  (winner's curse: E[V | you won] < E[V])
You pay the clearing price, not your bid, so bidding slightly above your value secures allocation cheaply — unless your bid is the one that sets the clearing price. Hence a ladder, with the weight at your value and a thin slice above it.
The generator commits 35% of cash: 20% of it in a thin slice 8% above shaded value to secure a fill, 55% at shaded value, 25% at 10% below as an opportunistic scoop. Edit freely.

Clearing simulator

Your read on the rest of the room, as a demand curve: at each price, how many shares the other houses want in total at or above it. Bids sort from the highest price down until 200,000 shares run out; everyone pays the last price needed, and ties at it share pro rata.

Doctrine

You are not maximising return. You are maximising R / max(D, 5%). A house at +15% with 8% drawdown scores 1.87. A house at +30% with 30% drawdown scores 1.00. The rulebook prints this example itself, which means the organisers expect most of the room to miss it.
  • Spend the free 5%, then stop. Below the floor, extra caution earns nothing. Above it, the first point costs the most.
  • Winning makes you more conservative, not less. Drawdown is measured from a running peak, so every credit earned shrinks the give-back you can afford.
  • Round-trip cost decides what you are allowed to trade. Bonds 0.10%, standard 0.40%, high-vol 0.60%, Frostwood 0.50%, Intricoin 1.00%. Intricoin needs a ten-times larger move than a bond to be worth touching — most of the room will day-trade it anyway because it is the coin.
  • Fade the room, trade the news. No headline on the board ⇒ it is someone's market impact ⇒ it reverts within about two minutes. Headline on the board ⇒ underlying value moved ⇒ do not fade it, and go buy the neighbours.
  • Bonds are the parking lot. 0.05% spread, 60M depth. Flattening into bonds is nearly free, which is what makes the hard stops actually executable.
  • The last 1.7 minutes are blind. Nobody sees any rank. Gambling into an unobservable leaderboard is pure variance — be flat or hedged, with the drawdown locked.

Session cards

S1 · Earnings season

  • Long only, cash only. The real job is calibration: log three fills of different sizes and fit k and α.
  • Confirm every volatility class and every sector off the board — the spillover matrix is worthless if the map is wrong.
  • Trade earnings beats via spillover, not via the stock that printed. The neighbours move 25% and the room is watching the headline name.

S2 · The Reserve decides

  • Odds: hike 40 / hold 45 / cut 15. Bonds are the clean expression — 0.05% spread means the cheapest way to hold a rate view.
  • Envelopes begin. Buy, do not open. Price them off the posterior table, and remember an opened envelope can never be resold.
  • If you do open: start the 120s clock and sit on your hands. 20% of trade value is not a fine, it is an amputation.

S3 · Zarhan votes

  • Shorts and 2× leverage unlock. Cap yourself at 1.4×.
  • Odds: reformist 55 / populist 45 — a near coin flip priced as a favourite. Pairs inside Zarhan beat an outright directional bet.
  • A populist win reviews the digital banks: ZAP and BZR down, Frostwood and SunVeld bid as savers run for safety.

S4 · The Ledgerly IPO

  • Bidding closes 81 seconds in. Value it in the first 40 seconds or do not bid.
  • Locked cash still counts in equity but cannot trade — do not lock the margin you need for the rest of the session.
  • If the clearing price lands above value, the price drifts back to value. Letting the room overpay without you is a perfectly good outcome.

S5 · The Strait talks

  • Live d20, no envelopes worth anything. Odds: deal 50 / stall 30 / restriction 20.
  • Options are long-only and expire at the close. Price the jump from the odds and compare to the smooth-vol quote.
  • Straddles are the purest expression — you are buying the jump size, and that is precisely what one lognormal cannot price.

S6 · The black swan

  • Hedge before the roll. Six faces, three of them named; make sure none of them craters you.
  • Hold the reroll if it is still unspent — this is the roll it was meant for.
  • Last 1.7 minutes: blind. Flatten, protect the peak, bank the score.

Envelope protocol

  • Buying is never flagged. Only opening starts a watch. So accumulate.
  • Two agreeing envelopes beat one by a mile. Run them through the posterior table — the second one is where the convexity is.
  • Open early, trade late. The watch is 120s; the reveal is mid-session. Opening the moment it lands means the watch is dead before the edge is live.
  • Unopened and expiring is still sellable until the reveal. Opened is worthless to everyone else, forever. If you do not intend to act, sell.
  • Forced liquidations are never flagged, and a house that never opens an envelope is never flagged at all.

Reroll policy

  • One per game, and the first passed vote burns it for everyone.
  • A mid-game roll going against you is not worth the reroll. Vote No, stay quiet; abstention counts as No and silence is a vote in your favour.
  • Push Yes only when a roll is genuinely existential, or when you are holding a position the fresh draw is likely to rescue.
  • Envelopes are not redone — a tip still names the outcome that was sealed. A reroll invalidates the tip you paid for.

Honours worth sniping

  • Steadiest hand — smallest drawdown among houses finishing in profit with 10+ trades. If you are playing the doctrine above you are already most of the way there. Just make sure you clear ten trades.
  • Best trade — largest profit booked on one closing trade. Concentrate a single high-conviction event trade and close it in one order rather than scaling out.
  • Most active desk — pure volume. Only chase it if bonds let you churn at 0.10% round trip without touching the drawdown.

Fair play — do not get clever

Scripts, bots and browser automation are prohibited, and an AI agent operating your desk counts as automation. A confirmed penalty costs 30% of your score's size and freezes the desk for 57 seconds of open trading, or gets you eliminated. This page is a calculator. It does not touch the terminal, and nothing here should ever be wired to one.
  • The bot watch flags trading patterns. Do not click at the 3/sec cap, do not place perfectly uniform slices at perfectly uniform intervals.
  • Ask the organisers, in writing, whether read-only price capture is permitted. If the answer is no, a human pastes the board.
  • Up to 3 logged-in devices. One of them is the desk, and only the trader touches it.

Desk roles

  • Board watcher — faces the newsboard and ticker. No laptop. Calls headlines with timestamps. This is the highest-value seat in the room and everyone will under-staff it.
  • Analyst — runs this page. Never touches the desk.
  • Trader — the only pair of hands on the terminal.
  • Risk officer — owns equity, drawdown, margin and the flatten veto.
